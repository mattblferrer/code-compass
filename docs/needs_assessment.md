## Programming Interview Practice

# Users and current situation
I spoke with three people preparing for software engineering and competitive programming interviews, namely, graduating students and students who are applying for internships. They use LeetCode, Codeforces, NeetCode lists, and company-tagged problems to prepare for technical screens, usually with an interview date or application season in mind. Other platforms such as AtCoder are less commonly used. 

I asked each to walk me through their usual practice session routine. Person A opened a curated list and worked through problems in order, but couldn’t say which patterns (sliding window, two pointers, etc.) they’d actually mastered. Person B had solved hundreds of problems but said that when he got a similar one two weeks later, he’d forgotten the approach. Person C kept an Excel spreadsheet of notes per problem and stopped updating it when interviews got close or when things got too busy in other aspects of life.

The main tools they relied on are LeetCode’s problem lists, tags, and difficulty labels, curated lists like Blind 75, spreadsheets, Notion, YouTube/NeetCode explanations, and friends who have interviewed recently.

# Main difficulty
People simply count solved problems, but can't tell what they're good or bad at. The volume of problems solved is being used as a stand-in for interview readiness. Also, many problems are solved with external help, such as guides or AI, which hides how much was actually learned independently.

Problems solved weeks ago are forgotten, and people rarely, if ever, visit them again. Solving once does not mean that they retain the algorithm knowledge or pattern recognition that the problem aims to instill.

With an interview deadline in mind, many people don't know which problems or areas to prioritize and simply grind randomly or follow a list blindly. Additionally, many people don't know the best resources to study to bolster their problem-solving skills. Without a clear plan, people don't make the best use of the limited time they have. 

With these needs in mind, LeetCode and Codeforces' problem sets are not the problem. Instead, the harder problems to solve are knowledge retention and problem recommendations.

# Need statement
People preparing for software engineering interviews need a way to see which problem patterns they have genuinely mastered and which they are forgetting or avoiding, so they can spend limited preparation time where it matters most before their interviews.

The design question is: How might we help interview candidates understand where they stand and decide what to practice next, given limited time?

# Desired improvement
Candidates would spend less time deciding what to practice and less time maintaining notes. They could see their strengths by pattern, which problems they solved alone versus with help, and which solved problems are due for review. With an interview date in mind, they could choose a plan such as “this week, review two-pointer problems I needed hints on, then try new graph problems,” and feel more confident about readiness.
