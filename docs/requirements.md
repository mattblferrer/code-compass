# CodeCompass: Interview Practice Guide – PRD

## 1. Project Overview

People preparing for software engineering interviews, such as graduating students and new applicants, have difficulty telling which problem patterns they have genuinely mastered and which they are forgetting, because practice sites count solved problems rather than retained problem-solving skill. This leaves limited preparation time spent on guesswork. My proposed product, CodeCompass, is a web app where the user logs coding-interview practice attempts, sees mastery by pattern, algorithm, or data structure, gets review reminders for problems solved with help or a long time ago, and receives a short, explained list of what to practice next. CodeCompass covers one user at a time, a compiled catalog of at least 500 commonly practiced interview problems, manual logging, CSV import, lookup of Codeforces and AtCoder problems by ID or link, and optional username-only sync and rating history for Codeforces, AtCoder, and LeetCode. It excludes running or judging code, hosting problem statements, logging in to practice sites, social features, and company-specific interview prediction.

## 3. Needs Assessment

People preparing for software engineering interviews need a way to see which problem patterns they have genuinely mastered and which they are forgetting or avoiding, so they can spend limited preparation time where it matters most before their interviews.

The design question is: How might we help interview candidates understand where they stand and decide what to practice next, given limited time?


## 4. Product Definition and Scope

*User need.* People preparing for software engineering interviews need a way to see which problem patterns they have genuinely mastered and which they are forgetting or avoiding, so they can spend limited preparation time where it matters most.

CodeCompass is a web app that gives an interview candidate a per-pattern view of their practice, a list of problems due for review, and a short, explained list of what to practice next. It supports the decision of where to spend the next practice session.

*What users will do.* After each practice problem, the candidate logs the problem and how it went: solved alone, solved with hints, solved after viewing the solution, or not solved. Before a session, they open the dashboard to see weak or fading patterns and the reviews that are due, then pick from the recommended problems, setting aside any that do not fit today with "not now." Due reviews are served as sibling problems from the same pattern. While solving, they can run a timer and, on a pattern they are new to, reveal a beginner hint. In a settings menu, they can enter upcoming interviews (each with a company name, a date, and an optional target difficulty and patterns), a starting difficulty, any target patterns (topics) they explicitly want to practice, any patterns they feel they have already mastered, whether to turn on advanced patterns (such as FFT or 2-SAT), whether to include very hard problems, and which sites' problems to recommend for core and for advanced patterns. Advanced patterns and very hard problems are off by default, and every site is shown by default. They can also link a Codeforces, AtCoder, or LeetCode username, sync their submissions, select whether they solved each one alone or with external help, and view their rating history on each linked site. To log a problem that is not in the catalog, they can enter its details or look it up by Codeforces or AtCoder ID or link. They can correct mistakes, export upcoming reviews to their calendar, and export their history.

*What it provides.* A mastery score for each pattern (or "not enough data"), a list of problems due for review with a banner when any are due, up to 5 recommended problems with a stated reason for each, a review streak, the time taken on each attempt, a calendar (.ics) export of review due dates, a list of synced submissions to confirm, rating history for linked sites, and a CSV export of the full history.

*How it addresses the difficulties.* Separating solved alone from solved with outside help addresses inflated solved counts that overstate the user's problem-solving skill; beginner hints count as help for the same reason. Review scheduling addresses forgotten problems, and serving reviews as sibling problems checks that the pattern is recognized rather than one problem remembered. The due-review banner and calendar export address reviews that would otherwise be missed, and a streak counted in review days gives a reason to return without rewarding volume. Pattern scores and reasoned recommendations address not knowing what to prioritize. Quick logging and the CSV import feature address notes and logging systems that get abandoned.

*Working conditions covered.* Self-paced practice on a laptop or phone browser (with the phone on the same Wi-Fi network as the laptop), logging soon after each problem, with or without an interview date, and no internet needed apart from opening problem links and the optional sync. 

*Included:* attempt logging (with outcome, optional time taken, and optional notes, finding the problem by title or by site and ID), an optional solve timer, a problem pattern classification system and problem catalog, beginner pattern hints, per-pattern mastery view, review scheduling with sibling-problem reviews, explained recommendations with optional interview dates for each company, target and self-marked mastered patterns, opt-in advanced patterns and very hard problems, a site filter for recommendations, and a "not now" option, a review-day streak, a due-review banner, entry editing and deletion, .ics export of review due dates, CSV import and export, custom problems with lookup by Codeforces or AtCoder ID or link, and optional username-only sync and rating history for Codeforces, AtCoder, and LeetCode.

*Excluded:* running, judging, or grading code; hosting problem statements or solutions; generating new or modified versions of problems; logging in to any practice site or storing its passwords or session cookies; LeetCode's full submission history; background syncing that the user did not start; scraping beyond the user-started requests in R-03; evading bot detection; resource recommendations (videos, courses); push or email notifications; using time taken in scores, review dates, or recommendations; social or group features; company-specific question prediction; user accounts with security hardening.
