# CodeCompass: Interview Practice Guide – PRD

## 1. Project Overview

People preparing for software engineering interviews, such as graduating students and new applicants, have difficulty telling which problem patterns they have genuinely mastered and which they are forgetting, because practice sites count solved problems rather than retained problem-solving skill. This leaves limited preparation time spent on guesswork. My proposed product, CodeCompass, is a web app where the user logs coding-interview practice attempts, sees mastery by pattern, algorithm, or data structure, gets review reminders for problems solved with help or a long time ago, and receives a short, explained list of what to practice next. CodeCompass covers one user at a time, a hand-built catalog of about 500 to 600 commonly practiced interview problems, and manual logging and CSV import. It excludes running or judging code, hosting problem statements, automatic syncing with practice sites, social features, and company-specific interview prediction.

## 2. Context and Starting Conditions

The 2- to 3-week timeline for a solo build means I will favor simple, explainable rules over machine learning, and spend most of my time on testing and a clean demo.

## 3. Needs Assessment

People preparing for software engineering interviews need a way to see which problem patterns they have genuinely mastered and which they are forgetting or avoiding, so they can spend limited preparation time where it matters most before their interviews.

The design question is: How might we help interview candidates understand where they stand and decide what to practice next, given limited time?


## 4. Product Definition and Scope

*User need.* People preparing for software engineering interviews need a way to see which problem patterns they have genuinely mastered and which they are forgetting or avoiding, so they can spend limited preparation time where it matters most.

CodeCompass is a web app that gives an interview candidate a per-pattern view of their practice, a list of problems due for review, and a short, explained list of what to practice next. It supports the decision of where to spend the next practice session.

*What users will do.* After each practice problem, the candidate logs the problem and how it went: solved alone, solved with hints, solved after viewing the solution, or not solved. Before a session, they open the dashboard to see weak or fading patterns and the reviews that are due, then pick from the recommended problems. They can enter an interview date, correct mistakes, and export their history.

*What it provides.* A mastery score for each pattern (or "not enough data"), a list of problems due for review, up to 5 recommended problems with a stated reason for each, and a CSV export of the full history.

*How it addresses the difficulties.* Separating solved alone from solved with outside help addresses inflated solved counts that overstate the user's problem-solving skill. Review scheduling addresses forgotten problems. Pattern scores and reasoned recommendations address not knowing what to prioritize. Quick logging and the CSV import feature address notes and logging systems that get abandoned.

*Working conditions covered.* Self-paced practice on a laptop or phone browser, logging soon after each problem, with or without an interview date, and no internet needed apart from opening problem links. 

*Included:* attempt logging (with outcome and optional notes), a problem pattern classification system and problem catalog, per-pattern mastery view, review scheduling, explained recommendations with an optional interview date, entry editing and deletion, and CSV import and export.

*Excluded:* running, judging, or grading code; hosting problem statements or solutions; automatic syncing or scraping of LeetCode, Codeforces, or other sites; resource recommendations (videos, courses); social or group features; company-specific question prediction; user accounts with security hardening.

## 5. Product Requirements Document (PRD)

### CodeCompass Requirements Table

| ID | Requirement | Why it is needed | What counts as passing | How you will check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-01 | [Inputs] The system must include a catalog of 500 to 600 interview problems, each with a title, link, difficulty (easy, medium, hard), and at least one pattern from a fixed list of at least 20 patterns. | Users cannot say which patterns they have mastered, so every problem needs a consistent pattern tag. The size is my estimate of what an 8-week preparation plan covers; I will revisit it after follow-up interviews. | Every catalog entry has all four fields, and every pattern is on the documented list. | Run a validation script over the catalog file, then compare 20 randomly chosen entries with the public lists they came from. |
| R-02 | [Inputs] The user must be able to add a custom problem with a title, difficulty, and at least one pattern, so problems from other sites can be logged. | Interviewees also practice on other platforms and lists, and the catalog will not cover everything. | A custom problem can be logged, and its attempts count toward the scores of its patterns. | In a test, add a custom problem, log an attempt, and compare the pattern score with a hand calculation. |
| R-03 | [Inputs] The system must not ask for credentials to other sites or send automated requests to them. | LeetCode has no public API, and scraping on these sites using user credentials create terms-of-service risk, which can lead to account bans and IP rate limits. | No credential fields for other sites exist, and no outgoing requests to practice sites appear. | Code review, plus a network log of a full demo run. |
| R-04 | [Logging] The system must record each attempt with the problem, date (default today), outcome (solved alone, solved with hints, solved after viewing the solution, or not solved), and optional notes. | Solved counts hide how much help was used, and the outcome field is what makes independent skill visible. | All 20 reference attempts reload with identical values. Twenty is a test-sized sample covering each outcome and several patterns. | Unit test: save and reload the reference attempts. |
| R-05 | [Logging errors] An attempt with no problem, no outcome, or a future date must not be saved, and a message must name the field to fix. | Wrong entries would distort scores and reduce the user's trust in the results. | For 6 invalid inputs (each of the three faults alone, and three combinations), nothing is saved and the message names the right field. | Unit tests with the 6 inputs, and a demonstration showing the messages. |
| R-06 | [Logging] Repeat attempts on the same problem must be stored as separate attempts and shown in date order. | Re-solving a problem after time has passed is the practice interviewees said they rarely do or track. | After 3 attempts on one problem, 3 records exist and the history lists them oldest to newest. | Unit test and demonstration. |
| R-07 | [Logging] The user must be able to edit or delete any attempt, and scores and due dates must update afterward. | Mistaken entries would reduce trust, and users should not need to start over. | After editing or deleting an attempt in a reference history, scores and due dates equal recalculated values. | Unit tests with recalculation. |
| R-08 | [Logging effort] Logging an attempt must take a median of 30 seconds or less for a first-time user. | Interviewees stopped updating spreadsheets and notes when time got tight. The 30 seconds is my provisional estimate that logging should be small next to a problem that takes tens of minutes. | Median time to log 5 attempts is 30 seconds or less across at least 3 users, or the revised target once set. | Timed user test with observation notes. |
| R-09 | [Mastery] The system must calculate a mastery score from 0 to 100 for each pattern from outcomes, number of attempts, and time since the last attempt, using a rule written down before testing. Here, mastery means estimated ability to solve the pattern without help, not interview readiness. | Users need to see which patterns are strong or weak instead of relying on a solved count. | For 5 reference histories (new user, many hints, long gap, mostly solved alone, mixed), scores match hand calculations within 0.01, the rounding tolerance for two decimals. | Unit tests compared with manual calculations. |
| R-10 | [Mastery errors] A pattern with too few attempts must show "not enough data" instead of a score. | A score from one or two attempts would mislead the user about where they stand. | In a sparse reference history, patterns below the minimum of 3 show "not enough data" and others show scores. | Unit test with a sparse history, and a demonstration. |
| R-11 | [Inspection] The user must be able to open any pattern and see the attempts behind its score. | Users need to judge whether a score is credible before acting on it. | For each pattern in the demo data, the attempts listed equal those used in the calculation. | Demonstration and comparison with the calculation inputs. |
| R-12 | [Review] The system must mark a solved problem as due for review on a schedule that is shorter for problems solved with hints or after viewing the solution. | Interviewees forget problems solved weeks earlier and rarely revisit them. | For reference histories, due dates match the documented intervals in 100% of cases, including month boundaries and time zone changes. Interval values are at least one week; they need spaced-repetition sources (for example SM-2) and tester feedback. | Unit tests with fixed dates. |
| R-13 | [Recommendations] The system must recommend up to 5 problems per request: review-due problems first, then new problems from the weakest patterns near the user's recent difficulty level. | Interviewees did not know what to prioritize and followed lists blindly. The limit of 5 is meant to fit one practice session. | For 5 reference histories, each list follows the documented order and never exceeds 5. | Rule-based tests against expected lists. |
| R-14 | [Recommendations] The system must not recommend a problem the user solved alone unless it is due for review. | Interviewees' time goes to problems they already know. | No such problem appears in the lists for the 5 reference histories. | Rule-based tests against expected lists. |
| R-15 | [Recommendations] When an interview date is entered, the system must weight recommendations toward the weakest patterns as fewer days remain, and must still work without a date. | Deadlines made interviewees unsure what to prioritize. | With the same history, entering a date changes the ranked list in the documented direction; with no date, a list is still produced. | Unit tests with and without a date. |
| R-16 | [Recommendations] Each recommendation must display the reason it was chosen (for example, "two pointers: solved with hints 12 days ago"). | Users who follow lists blindly need reasons to judge advice. | Every recommendation in the 5 reference histories shows a non-empty reason that matches the rule that selected it. | Workflow demonstration and inspection of the output. |
| R-17 | [Results] The dashboard must show the score or "not enough data" for every pattern, and the number of problems due for review, on one screen. | Users need to see where they stand at a glance before choosing what to practice. | With seeded demo data, all patterns appear with values equal to the calculated ones. | Demonstration and comparison with the calculations. |
| R-18 | [Results] A user must be able to identify their two weakest patterns and one recommended next problem from the dashboard in 2 minutes or less. | The purpose of the product is better decisions about what to practice. Two minutes is a provisional "quick look before a session" target. | At least 80% of task attempts are correct across 3 to 5 testers. | Observed user test with a task script. |
| R-19 | [Data exchange] The system must export all attempts as CSV and import a CSV with the same columns. | Interviewees already keep spreadsheets or notes, and should not be locked in. | Exporting and re-importing 100 reference attempts gives identical records. One hundred is a test-sized sample. | Round-trip test. |
| R-20 | [Data exchange errors] When a CSV has invalid rows, the system must import the valid rows and report each skipped row with its row number and reason. | Imported spreadsheets are often messy, and silently dropped rows would hide missing history. | For a 10-row file with 3 invalid rows, 7 rows are imported and the 3 skipped rows are listed with reasons. | Test with the 10-row file. |
| R-21 | [Performance] The dashboard and recommendations must load in 2 seconds or less with 500 logged attempts on the target laptop. | A page that makes users wait discourages quick checks. The 500 is my estimate of a heavy user (about 5 problems a day for 100 days) and the 2 seconds is a responsiveness rule of thumb; neither comes from my interviews yet. | Measured load time is 2 seconds or less across 10 runs. | Timing test on the target laptop. |
| R-22 | [Conditions] The system must keep saved data after the app restarts. | Users build a history over weeks, and losing it would end use. | After restart, all reference attempts, scores, and due dates are unchanged. | Smoke test: save, restart, compare. |
| R-23 | [Conditions] The main flows (log, dashboard, recommendations) must work in current Chrome and Firefox on desktop and at a phone width of 375 px. | Interviewees practice on laptops and log from phones. The 375 px is the width of common small phones. | The main flows complete in both browsers and at 375 px with no content cut off. | Demonstration on two browsers and one phone. |
| R-24 | [Conditions] The core features (logging, dashboard, review, recommendations) must work with no internet access, apart from opening problem links. | Free hosting can sleep or reset, so the demo needs a local fallback, and users practice in varied places. | With the network disabled, the full reference flow completes. | Disable the network and run the demo flow. |
| R-25 | [Conditions] The system must include a demo dataset of at least 8 weeks of synthetic practice history. | A 2 to 3 week project cannot collect long-term history. Eight weeks is my assumed typical preparation window and is long enough for the longer review intervals to fall due. | Loading the demo dataset populates the dashboard, due reviews, and recommendations. | Demonstration. |
